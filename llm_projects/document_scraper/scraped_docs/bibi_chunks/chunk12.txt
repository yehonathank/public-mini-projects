During President Obama's Cairo speech on 4 June 2009 in
which Obama addressed the Muslim world, Obama stated,
among other things, "The United States does not accept the
legitimacy of continued Israeli settlements. " Following
Obama's Cairo speech Netanyahu immediately called a
special government meeting. [104] On 14 June, ten days after
Obama's Cairo speech, Netanyahu gave a speech at Bar-Ilan
University in which he endorsed a "Demilitarized Palestinian
State". [105] Netanyahu stated that he would accept a
Palestinian state if Jerusalem were to remain the united
capital of Israel, the Palestinians would have no army, and the
Palestinians would give up their demand for a right of return. He also argued the right for a "natural growth" in the existing
Jewish settlements in the West Bank while their permanent
status is up to further negotiation. [104] The speech was broadcast live in Israel and across parts of the

Arab world. He endorsed for the first time the notion of a Palestinian state alongside Israel. [106] He stated
that he would be willing to meet with any "Arab leader" for negotiations without preconditions,
specifically mentioning Syria, Saudi Arabia, and Lebanon. [106] The address represented a new position
for Netanyahu's government on the peace process. [107]
Right-wing members of Netanyahu's governing coalition criticized his remarks for the creation of a
Palestinian State, believing all of the land should come under Israeli sovereignty. Likud MK Danny
Danon said Netanyahu went "against the Likud platform",[108] MK Uri Orbach of Habayit Hayehudi said
it had "dangerous implications". [109] Opposition party Kadima leader Tzipi Livni opined that Netanyahu
did not really believe in the two-state solution and that his speech was a response to international
pressure. [110]
Netanyahu's speech provoked mixed reaction internationally. [111] The Palestinian National Authority
rejected the conditions on a Palestinian State. [112] Senior Palestinian official Saeb Erekat said that the
speech had "closed the door to permanent status negotiations" due to Netanyahu's declarations on
Jerusalem, refugees and settlements. [104] Hamas spokesman Fawzi Barhoum said it was "racist"[112] and
called on Arab nations to "form stronger opposition". [107] The Arab League dismissed the speech,
declaring that "Arabs would not make concessions regarding issues of Jerusalem and refugees". [113] The
Czech Republic Foreign Minister Jan Kohout, whose country held the EU's presidency at the time, said
". this is a step in the right direction. The acceptance of a Palestinian state was present there".
