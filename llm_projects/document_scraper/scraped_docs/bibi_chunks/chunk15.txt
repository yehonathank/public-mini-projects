Netanyahu unsuccessfully called for the early release of
Jonathan Pollard, an American serving a life sentence for
passing secret U. S. documents to Israel in 1987. [151] He has
raised the issue at the Wye River Summit in 1998, where he
claimed that U. S. president Bill Clinton had privately agreed
to release Pollard. [152][153] In 2002, Netanyahu visited Pollard
at his North Carolina prison. [154][155] The Israeli prime
minister maintained contact with Pollard's wife, and was
active in pressing the Obama administration to release
Pollard. [156][157]
In 2011, social justice protests broke out across Israel. Hundreds of thousands of people protested Israel's high cost
of living throughout the country. In response, Netanyahu
appointed the Trajtenberg Committee, headed by professor Manuel Trajtenberg, to examine the problems
and propose solutions. The committee submitted recommendations to lower the high cost of living in
September 2011. [158] Although Netanyahu promised to push the proposed reforms through the cabinet in
one piece, differences inside his coalition resulted in the reforms being gradually adopted.
