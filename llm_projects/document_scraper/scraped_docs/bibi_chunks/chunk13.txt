President Obama's press secretary, Robert Gibbs, said the speech was an "important step

Three months after starting his term, Netanyahu remarked that his cabinet already had achieved several
successes, such as the establishment of a working national unity government, and a broad consensus for a
"two-state solution". [116] A July 2009 survey by Ha'aretz found that most Israelis supported the
Netanyahu government, giving him a personal approval rating of about 49 percent. [117] Netanyahu has
lifted checkpoints in the West Bank in order to allow freedom of movement and a flow of imports; a step
that resulted in an economic boost in the West Bank. [118][119][120] In 2009, Netanyahu welcomed the Arab
Peace initiative (also known as the "Saudi Peace Initiative") and lauded a call by Bahrain's Crown Prince
Salman bin Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa to normalize relations with Israel. [121][122]
On 9 August 2009, speaking at the opening of a government meeting, Netanyahu said: "We want an
agreement with two factors, the first of which is the recognition of Israel as the national state of the
Jewish people and (the second of which is) a security settlement". [123] In August 2009, Palestinian
Authority chairman Mahmoud Abbas declared that he would be willing to meet with Netanyahu at the
UN General Assembly, where Netanyahu had accepted president Obama's invitation for a "triple
summit", although he said it would not necessarily lead to negotiations. [124] Netanyahu was reported to
be in a pivotal moment over these understandings, that were reported to include a compromise over
permission on continuing the already approved construction in the West Bank in exchange for freezing all
settlements thereafter, as well as continuing building in East Jerusalem, and at the same time stopping the
demolition of houses of Arab inhabitants there. [125] On 4 September 2009, it was reported that Netanyahu
was to agree to settlers' political demands to approve more settlement constructions before a temporary
settlement freeze agreement took place. [126] White House spokesman Robert Gibbs expressed "regret"
over the move;[127] however, one U. S. official said the move will not "derail [the] train". [128]
On 7 September 2009, Netanyahu left his office without reporting his destination. The prime minister's
military secretary later reported Netanyahu had visited a security facility in Israel. [129] Various news
agencies reported different stories about his whereabouts. [130] On 9 September 2009, Yedioth Ahronoth
reported that the Israeli leader had made a secret flight to Moscow to try to persuade Russian officials not
to sell S-300 anti-aircraft missile systems to Iran. [129][131][132] Headlines branded Netanyahu a "liar" and
dubbed the affair a "fiasco". [133][134] The PM's military secretary was later reportedly dismissed due to
the affair.
