On 24 September 2009, in an address to the UN General Assembly in New York, Netanyahu said Iran
poses a threat to the peace of the world and that it is incumbent on the world body to prevent the Islamic
Republic from obtaining nuclear weapons. [137][138] Waving the blueprints for Auschwitz and invoking the
memory of his own family members murdered by the Nazis, Netanyahu delivered a passionate and public
riposte to Iranian president Mahmoud Ahmadinejad's questioning of the Holocaust, asking: "Have you no
shame?"[139]
In response to pressure from the Obama administration urging the resumption of peace talks, on 25
November 2009 Netanyahu announced a partial 10-month settlement construction freeze plan. The
announced partial freeze had no significant effect on actual settlement construction, according to an
analysis by Haaretz. [140] U. S. special envoy George Mitchell said, "while the United States shares Arab
concerns about the limitations of Israel's gesture, it is more than any Israeli government has ever
done". [141] In his announcement Netanyahu called the move "a painful step that will encourage the peace
process" and urged the Palestinians to respond. [142] The Palestinians rejected the call, stating the gesture
was "insignificant" in that thousands of recently approved settlement buildings in the West Bank would
continue to be built and there would be no freeze of settlement activity in East Jerusalem. [143]
In March 2010, Israel's government approved construction of an additional 1,600 apartments in a large
Jewish housing development in northern East Jerusalem called Ramat Shlomo[144] despite the position of
the current U. S. Government that acts such as this thwart the peace talks. The Israeli government's
announcement occurred during a visit by U. S. Vice-president Joe Biden and the U. S. government issued a
strongly worded condemnation of the plan. [145] Netanyahu issued a statement that all previous Israeli
governments had continuously permitted construction in the neighborhood, and that certain
neighborhoods such as Ramat Shlomo and Gilo have always been included as part of Israel in any final
agreement plan that has been proposed by either side to date. [144] Netanyahu regretted the timing of the
announcement but asserted that "our policy on Jerusalem is the same policy followed by all Israeli
governments for the 42 years, and it has not changed. "[146]
In September 2010, Netanyahu agreed to enter direct talks, mediated by the Obama administration, with
the Palestinians.
